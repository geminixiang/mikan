mikan has one sandbox, `oci`: it creates and manages one container per conversation office, with that office's workspace mounts, vault env and files, and resource limits. `--sandbox=oci` uses the image published for the installed mikan version, `--sandbox=oci:<image>` uses another image, and without `--sandbox` mikan uses `oci`. A container engine (nerdctl, Podman, or Docker) must be available on the host.

One conversation has one vault and one container. The vault is named by the office key: the versioned `v1-<platform>-<readable-id>-<hash>` segment that also names the conversation's directory under the workspace. See [Conversation offices](#conversation-offices).

<Aside type="note" title="What the sandbox is for">
  `oci:*` targets a team sharing one VM. Each office gets resource limits, an isolated filesystem,
  and its own environment. It is not a hostile multi-tenant boundary like E2B or a SaaS sandbox:
  containers share the host kernel and everyone on the VM is trusted. Credentials enter the sandbox
  only because development work (`gh`, `gcloud`, `gws`) needs them.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Mode names">
  `host`, `container:<name>`, and `docker:*` are not sandbox modes, and mikan refuses them at
  startup. Use `oci` or `oci:<image>`.
</Aside>

## Conversation offices

Each conversation owns one directory under the workspace root — its _office_ — named by office key
rather than by the platform's raw conversation id. Sandbox mounts follow that directory, so inside
a runtime the office is at `/workspace/<office-key>`.

Which parts of the workspace a runtime sees follows the office's **visibility** (ADR 0008), derived
from the Slack conversation type: public channels are public; private channels, DMs, group DMs,
externally shared channels, and unknown kinds are private. Every office gets the same mount shape;
only the read-only flags differ:

| Mounted under `/workspace`                   | Public office | Private office |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------- | -------------- |
| `<office-key>/` — the office itself          | read-write    | read-write     |
| `MEMORY.md`, `skills/` — shared knowledge    | read-write    | read-only      |
| `public/<office-key>/` — every public office | read-only     | read-only      |

Nothing mounts the workspace root. The admin portal can narrow a
public channel; nothing can widen beyond Slack. Field
semantics and the retired door-policy settings are documented in [Configuration](/configuration/).

<Aside type="caution" title="Visibility is not an extra sandbox">
  Visibility decides which host directories are projected into the runtime. It does not change
  execution isolation: every office runs in its own container whatever its visibility.
</Aside>

### Upgrading from the raw-id layout

0.5.3 workspaces hold directories named by raw conversation id. `mikan migrate` moves them to the
office layout, together with their vaults, settings, sessions, and events; see
[Upgrading from 0.5.3](/deployment/#upgrading-from-053).
